shuffle productions. It binds relative possession to the typed participant,
retains player/card target cardinality and destination, and rejects unknown

## Native original and completion ownership

The old whole-graveyard loop now reuses the native object-shuffle owner. It
preserves exact source-zone snapshots, actual movement counts, commander
alternatives and rollback. Existing cryptographic library operations remain the
randomization authority. Every participant's physical shuffle belongs to the
original action and precedes appended replacement programs. Every physical
shuffle receives distinct event provenance.

One shared resource transaction and trigger-matching scope span native
preparation and completion. Exact zone receipts and completed arrival bindings
freeze before another continuation can move those arrivals. A genuinely
unfinished replacement arrival freezes at its own completion. Prefix events
and facts appear once; retained continuations do not replay prefixes.

## Bounded custom-program limitation

An arbitrary AsEntersProgram containing a draw, or a synthetic replacement that
creates a draw inside it, still needs the typed entry matcher/program state
retained across suspension. Terminal entry-Instead payload retention alone does
not cover that custom-program surface. A source search of the pinned
`cards-20261003.json.xz` found no direct as-enters draw instruction or concrete
supported as-enters interaction that produces one. Existing compiler examples
cover exile, mill, sacrifice, return, counter removal and choice/setup;
ordinary ETB draw triggers wait on the stack. This limitation is recorded
separately from the eight reviewed printed bodies and does not claim blanket
executor completeness.
